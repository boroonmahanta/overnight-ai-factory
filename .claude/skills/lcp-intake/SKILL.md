# Intake (GET AI)

Adapted from `triage` by Matt Pocock (MIT, pinned at `mattpocock/skills` commit `4ae2317ddb22b046e26455e4f9c9b3aaaf62212d`, originals in `skills/pocock/triage/` including `AGENT-BRIEF.md` and `OUT-OF-SCOPE.md`). Changed: GitHub labels replaced by ADO states and fields, a size and a Merge Danger floor added, and the night rules added. The machine triage of client reports is a different skill: `lcp-triage`.

A human decides. This skill helps them. It never sets Factory Eligible (rule 1 and plan 6.4).

Every comment you post in ADO starts with:

```
> *This was generated by AI during intake.*
```

## What arrives

- Draft bugs the orchestrator filed from `lcp-triage` (tag `triage-inbox`, state New).
- New requests from people (state New).
- Items in Refining that need more.

## Steps for one item

1. **Gather context.** Read the whole item: body, comments, tags, linked reports, evidence, `triage.json` if there is one. Parse earlier notes so you do not ask what is already answered. Look up the area in `glossary.md` and the decision records. Run two checks: **already built?** (search by what it does, not the wording, and say where you looked) and **rejected before?** (read the out-of-scope records, below).
2. **Recommend.** Give a category (bug or change), a state, a proposed **size** (XS to XL, largest answer wins) and a Merge Danger floor, with reasons. Wait for the human.
3. **Verify the claim.** For a bug, confirm the repro from the evidence in `factory-test` (using `lcp-verify` when it exists). Report: confirmed (with the path), failed, or not enough detail. A confirmed repro makes a much stronger brief.
4. **Grill if needed.** If it needs fleshing out, ask one question at a time. Facts are looked up, decisions are asked, by day only (rule 9). Update `glossary.md` terms and decision records as decisions land.
5. **Apply the outcome.**
   - **Ready to build:** write the brief (below) and hand to the human to set Factory Eligible once locked scenarios, held-out set, mocks and prerequisites exist.
   - **Needs a person:** write what we know and the exact questions (`lcp-questionnaire` if the answer sits with someone else). State Refining.
   - **Foggy or too big (XL):** open a Wayfinder map, do not split by guessing.
   - **Close:** say why. Already built: point to where. Rejected change: write an out-of-scope record. Duplicate: link and merge.

## The story brief

The brief is the contract the night agent works from. It may sit for days while the solution changes, so it has to stay true.

- **Describe behaviour and interfaces, not paths.** Name tables, columns, flows and agents by their logical names. Do not give screen positions or line numbers.
- **Say what, not how.** The builder explores and decides.
- **Acceptance criteria that can each be checked alone**, matching the locked scenarios.
- **Out of scope**, so nothing gets gold-plated.
- Template: **Category, Summary, Current behaviour, Desired behaviour, Key interfaces, Acceptance criteria, Out of scope**, plus **Size**, **Merge Danger floor**, **Residency tier**, **Prerequisites** and **Mock or prototype link** for user-facing work.

A bad brief says "fix the approval flow, it is broken", names a screen, and has no criteria.

## Out-of-scope records

Keep `docs/out-of-scope/<concept>.md`, one file per **concept**, not per ticket. Each has the decision, a real reason that will still hold in a year, and links to every request. Write one only when a **change** is rejected, never for a bug and never for something already built. Match new requests by meaning ("night theme" matches "dark mode") and show the old reason to the human before closing.

## Quick override

If the human says "move it to X", confirm what you are about to do, then do it. Do not argue. If they skip grilling, ask once whether they want a brief written.

## Resuming

If earlier notes exist, read them, check whether anyone answered, and show an updated picture. Do not re-ask what is settled.
