In one minute for everyone

The factory turns approved specs into tested pull requests while we sleep. People still decide what to build and what ships. Agents do the build and the checking in between.

People Decide
  • Humans own the idea, the spec and every merge to main.
  • Someone other than the builder writes the tests and locks them.
  • A human ticks Factory Eligible before any story runs at night.
Agents Build Overnight
  • One story per lane, on one branch, with one PR.
  • The agent looks up facts itself and stops to ask on decisions.
  • Steps that need a portal or a login wait for a human.
The Gate Decides Done
  • The agent never marks its own work. A separate gate checks it.
  • Fresh AI verifiers click through the app in a Test environment.
  • A risk score from 0 to 10 sets the checks and who approves.

Ceremony matches the stakes. Trust is earned on evidence, never on a date.

When a story leaves the main path

Awaiting business

The agent PARKed. It hit a portal step or a decision, wrote exact instructions and stopped. A human does the step, notes it in the work item, and sets the story back to Ready to build. The next night resumes from the branch.

Back to Building

The gate said NEEDS_WORK. The story goes to the next free lane with the findings, two rounds at most.

Needs rework

Caps used up, or a human rejected the PR.

PARK types: portal_step, design_approval, ambiguity, one_way_door, budget. Before an ambiguity PARK the agent must check the code, Dataverse metadata, ADO history, the glossary and decision records.

Before anyone ticks Factory Eligible

  • The spec passed the gate script, with no open markers and every checklist ticked.
  • Locked scenarios exist, written by someone other than the builder, with their hash recorded.
  • A held-out counterpart exists in factory-holdout.
  • Any user-facing change has an approved mock or prototype linked.
  • Every OAuth or consent step is set up, or listed as a PARK step.
  • The client's residency tier allows factory work.
  • The verify profile and glossary changes the spec lists are merged.
  • For a triaged bug, a human has read the linked evidence.

The orchestrator rejects the flag if any item is missing.

Which lane does my work take?

Same rules, different engines. The lane is a field in the spec header. The preset sets it and the gate checks it. Power Platform comes first; the code lanes follow at stages 5 and 6.

Both squads share
  • Specs are shaped by day on Spec Kit 1.2.
  • Scenarios come from someone else and are locked.
  • One gate design and the Merge Danger score.
  • One PR body: what was built, a diagram, before and after evidence.

  • Every run has a run_id, a credit cap and a loop cap.

Merge Danger and the trust ladder

Every PR gets a risk score from 0 to 10. A script sets the floor. AI can raise the score, never lower it. The score decides how many verifiers run and who must approve.

1. Floor
A trusted script reads the changed paths, the schema and security plans, and the gate results.
2. Review
Two fresh AI reviewers check standards and spec. Each may raise the score, with a reason.
3. Final
The highest of the three. Nothing lowers the floor.

How a change class climbs

  • Rungs are set per project and per change class, such as views-forms-additive or flow-logic.
  • To reach L2: 30 days of defect data, 80% evaluator agreement with humans for four weeks, and under 2% reverted.
  • Also no tampering, a stable held-out pass rate, and a written sign-off from Boroon.
  • Any escaped defect drops the class back to L1 at once.
  • main is always human, at every rung.

Your rhythm as an engineer

The factory learns from your corrections. The weekly retro turns them into skills, lints and standards, always by a reviewed PR.

Now, stages 0 to 2

  • Keep using the AI by day in VS Code and Copilot. That is rung L0.
  • Your PRs go through the gate once it is live on your repo.
  • Notice where you correct the agent. Those corrections become the real process.

By day

  • Run the Spec Kit day gate with the spec owner.
  • Write or review scenarios, then lock them.
  • Check triage evidence before a bug goes to the gate.

Each morning

  • Read the evidence first, then the code.
  • Resolve PARKs and approve PRs into integration.
  • Read one failed trace in full, and log your review minutes.

Each week, 90 minutes

  • Grade 10 verifier verdicts blind and read the traces behind each disagreement.
  • Retro on 5 to 10 real sessions, some that failed and some that looked fine.
  • Seed a fake violation and check the gate catches it.

Before the night shift

  • Export a sample of your own day sessions, by choice.
  • Remove client data first.
  • The retro mines them for repeated corrections, such as "ask before deleting".

Each model release

  • Remove one piece of glue the new model may not need.
  • Measure, then keep it out or put it back.
  • Re-check after Microsoft Ignite, 17 to 20 November.
80%

Human minutes are about 80% of a story's cost in the plan's model, about USD 150 per accepted story fully loaded. Clear specs, good scenarios and easy reviews move the margin far more than token prices do.

Rules and words you will hear

The plan has 19 rules we do not break. These 13 matter most day to day. The numbers are the plan's own, from section 2.3.

Rules we do not break

  1. 1Humans own intent, spec approval and every merge to main. No agent or App merges to main.
  2. 2The worker never decides it is done. The gate and an isolated evaluator decide.
  3. 3Scenarios come from someone other than the worker, locked before the night.
  4. 4A held-out set lives where the worker cannot read it. Only pass rates come back.
  5. 5Credentials and untrusted code never share a job.
  6. 7One story, one lane, one branch, one PR, under a lane lock.
  7. 8Code over click-ops. A step that needs the portal or a consent PARKs.
  8. 9Facts are looked up; decisions are asked. Clarify by day only.
  9. 10Design first. An approved mock or throwaway prototype. Prototypes are never promoted.
  10. 11The Merge Danger floor is deterministic. AI may raise the score, never lower it.
  11. 12Auto-merge is a rung, not a phase. Only into integration, per class, on evidence.
  12. 15Client code and data follow the client's residency tier. No client production data in lanes.
  13. 17Prompts, agents, skills, gate scripts and standards change only by a reviewed daytime PR.

Words you will hear

Lane
A sandbox environment that runs one story at a time, under a lock. Three for Power Platform.
Gate
A required workflow in a separate repo the agent cannot change. It decides whether work is done.
Locked scenarios
Acceptance tests written by someone other than the builder and fixed before the night.
Held-out set
A second set of tests the agent never sees. Only pass rates come back.
Verifier
A fresh AI session that clicks through the app in Test to check the work.
PARK
The agent stops at a decision or a portal step and leaves exact instructions for a human.
Factory Eligible
The ADO flag a human ticks when a story is ready for the night.
One-way door
A change that is hard to undo, such as dropped data or a new security role.
Blast radius
Every table, flow, app and agent a change touches, and who uses them.
integration
The branch factory work merges into. main moves only by a human release PR.
lcp-verify
The agent's hands and eyes: it opens apps, queries tables, triggers flows and runs agent tests.
Wayfinder map
A map of open questions for foggy work. When every child item closes, it becomes the spec.
Spec Kit
GitHub's open-source kit for spec-driven work. We use version 1.2 with GET AI presets.
